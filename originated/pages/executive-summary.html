Executive Summary

Build date: 2026-11-13 · Season: 2026-27 · Window: Fri 13 Nov – Sun 15 Nov 2026 (23 games) · Session: session-aaa8b341

1. What this system is

NBA2 originates its own NBA betting lines rather than consuming a sportsbook's. It reads official NBA data, turns it into team power ratings, projects a margin and a total for every game on the slate, and converts those into a fair spread, total and moneyline. On top of that fair line sits a catalog of strategies, each of which is allowed to claim an edge only if it uses information that is not already baked into the line.

Slates are produced up to 3 days ahead, which is the horizon the official NBA schedule and the official NBA injury report jointly support.

4. Headline findings

  1. The league's home-court advantage this season is tiny. Derived from the home/away splits

    2. across all 30 teams: 89‑84 (.5145), which implies only +0.53 points of home court. The multi-season NBA norm is usually quoted far higher. With 173 split games the standard error is about ±3.8 percentage points, so this estimate is unstable, and it is the single largest source of error in the originated spreads. The sensitivity panel re-prices the whole board at +0.5 / +1.5 / +2.5 points so the effect is visible rather than hidden.

  1. Two teams are extreme outliers and dominate the board. Chicago (1‑9, −15.5 PPG) and

    2. Milwaukee (1‑11, −14.5) are more than 8 points per game worse than the next-worst team. Sacramento (1‑10, −12.7) and Utah (1‑11, −11.5) are close behind in the West. Four teams this bad is unusual and it makes several originated spreads very large (largest on the board: CHI @ MIN, MIN −16).

  1. **The official injury report is the richest verified signal on the board, but most of it is

    2. noise for betting purposes. Of the 91 player rows, a large share are G League two-way and G League assignment entries — players who were not in the NBA rotation anyway. The index that actually carries information is the rotation-availability index**, which excludes them. Counting G League rows as rotation losses (the obvious mistake, and one this build initially made) changes the picture materially: for example it made Oklahoma City look healthier than the Clippers when the reverse is true once G League rows are stripped out.

  1. Same-game parlays on winner × total are priced essentially fairly by independence.

    2. Measured across 23 games with 200,000 simulations each, the largest binary correlation (φ) between a moneyline leg and a totals leg is 0.0136. The independence assumption a book uses to price those parlays is off by well under a percentage point. The dangerous correlation is the one books already restrict: moneyline × spread-cover, φ ≈ 0.80.

  1. Parlays compound the vig, and that is arithmetic, not opinion. At the 4.77% overround

    2. implied by a −110/−110 two-way market, a k‑leg parlay built from fair legs loses 4.55% (1 leg) → 8.90% (2) → 13.05% (3) → 17.00% (4). A parlay is only rational if each leg clears its own break-even by roughly the overround.

  1. No strategy clears every validation gate. Eight of ten pass 6–7 of 8 gates. **Gate G6

    2. (out-of-sample validation) fails for all ten, because this repository contains no graded historical bet log. That is the honest state of the project, and it is the reason the leaderboard is labelled model-expected, not realised**.

5. The two blockers

Everything else on this site is usable today. These two are not, and they are the highest-value things to unblock:

  • Q‑01 — no market price feed. We originate fair lines, but "edge" only exists relative to a

    • price someone will actually give you. Without a market feed the system cannot compute a bettable edge, only a fair price. See Open Questions.

  • Q‑02 — no official per-player feed. stats.nba.com and cdn.nba.com are unreachable from

    • this build environment, so no player prop threshold is published anywhere on this site. Every prop cell is deliberately blank and flagged, rather than filled with an estimate.
