Luck versus skill

A hit rate is not evidence. These are the controls that turn it into one — and the controls that show where this sample still cannot answer the question.

Finding: the model does not beat the obvious rule. On the same 139 games, "always take the team with the better point differential" scores 64.0% and the originated model scores 62.6%. The difference is -1.44% (permutation p = 0.90). The model's complexity is not yet paying for itself.

All three rules are point-in-time: the differential rule uses only games before each tip-off. Comparing a model with a coin flip instead of with this rule is how sports models claim skill they do not have.

A 1.09-point improvement with p = 0.35 is consistent with luck. It is reported as an improvement in the point estimate and not as a demonstrated edge.

A 1-point edge needs roughly 4,500 settled games to detect. The 139 games graded here cannot rank strategies, and no amount of presentation changes that arithmetic.

What is still impossible to answer

  • Is any strategy profitable? Unknowable: no prices. ROI would be fiction.
  • Would this beat a market line? Unknowable for the same reason. Beating our own line measures self-consistency only.
  • Is the champion the best? No: the 139-game sample is below the G-01 floor of 300 settled bets.
