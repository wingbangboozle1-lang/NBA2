Leaderboard — measured, luck-adjusted, reward withheld

Ranked on a deflated z-score against a coin flip, with a search penalty, so a hot week cannot win. Rewards are gated, not given.

No reward is paid. Every strategy's reward is 0 because G-01 fails (the sample is far below the G-01 floor of 300 settled bets) and only reliable passes count toward the score. The pool stays unallocated by design.

Scoring rule

  1. z = (accuracy − 0.5) / √(0.25 / n). It measures how far above a coin flip a strategy sits, in standard errors — not in wins.
  2. Deflation. z is divided by √k where k = 4 registered strategies. Trying many strategies and keeping the best one is the most common way to manufacture a champion.
  3. Reward gate. 0 unless every registerable gate passes; then proportional to the deflated z above 1.0, scaled by the fraction of gates passed.
  4. Pre-registration. Each strategy is mapped to one confidence tier before outcomes are read, so it cannot be re-labelled onto whichever tier did well.

Why this ranking cannot be trusted yet

  • Ranked on deflated z, not on win rate. 164 graded games is far below the G-01 floor of 300 settled bets, so the ordering is provisional by construction.
  • Reward units are 0 for every strategy while any validation gate is FAILED. That is the gate doing its job, not a missing feature.
  • Strategies are mapped to confidence tiers before outcomes are seen, so a strategy cannot be re-labelled onto whichever tier happened to perform.
  • Every strategy here shares one model. Their results are therefore highly correlated and the leaderboard compares *filters on the same forecasts*, not independent systems.
