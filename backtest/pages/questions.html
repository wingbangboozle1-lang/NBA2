A. Answerable now, from this session's work

Questions answered on this page's evidence
#QuestionAnswerEvidence
Q1Does the originated model beat a coin flip?Yes, in the point estimate (62.6% straight-up, AUROC 0.70), but the interval is wide and prices are unavailable, so this is not a bettable edge.Luck vs Skill
Q2Does it beat the obvious rule?No. 62.6% versus 64.0% for "take the better point differential" (p = 0.90).Luck vs Skill
Q3Does it beat a constant line?Yes on margin error (12.17 vs 13.27 pts MAE) but not significantly (p = 0.35).Luck vs Skill
Q4Is the model's total any good?No. Total MAE 15.78 versus 15.43 for simply predicting the league-average total. The total component is currently worse than a constant.Walk-Forward
Q5Are same-game legs correlated?Not detectably: r(margin, total) = -0.009 (95% CI -0.17 to 0.15). The same-game parlay premise is unsupported.Correlation
Q6Do back-to-backs hurt?Directionally yes (-1.54 vs +1.27 pts) but not significantly (p = 0.38).Correlation
Q7Are the tiers informative?Partly: Tier A 75.4% vs Tier C 46.2% straight-up. Tier C's interval includes a coin flip, which is what a noise tier should look like.Walk-Forward
Q8How much data is needed before a leaderboard means anything?Roughly 300 settled bets to see a 3-point edge; about 4,551 to see a 1-point edge at 80% power.Luck vs Skill
