Injuries → rotation minutes → production

Availability is the single largest driver of short-term NBA line movement. This page pins down the league's official reporting rules (verified verbatim), explains what each designation does and does not tell you, and defines the minutes-redistribution framework that turns an injury report into line and prop adjustments.

Official reporting policy (verified verbatim)

RuleVerified text / valueSource
What must be reported"NBA teams must report information concerning player injuries, illnesses, other medical conditions, or rest for all NBA games."official.nba.com (2026-27)
First deadline"By 5 p.m. local time the day before a game (other than the second day of a back-to-back)" teams must designate a participation status and identify a specific reason.official.nba.com
Back-to-back exception"For the second game of a back-to-back, teams must report the above information by 1 p.m. local time on the day of the game."official.nba.com
Game-day updatesRequired between 11 a.m. and 1 p.m. local time (8–10 a.m. for tip-offs 5 p.m. or earlier), except second game of a back-to-back.official.nba.com
Update cadence"Reports are updated on a continual basis throughout the day" — the league publishes timestamped PDFs (e.g., 15-minute increments).official.nba.com
Report formatColumns: Game Date, Game Time, Matchup, Team, Player Name, Current Status, Reason — statuses include Available / Questionable / Doubtful / Out / Probable; reasons include Injury/Illness, Rest, G League - Two-Way, Personal Reasons, Not With Team.official report PDF sample
Reading the designations honestly: the NBA publishes statuses and reasons — it does not publish probabilities attached to them. Any mapping like "Questionable ≈ 50%" is folklore, not official. Our framework treats designations as states of uncertainty and estimates availability probabilities from each player's own history — a value we compute and label INTERNAL, never an official fact.
