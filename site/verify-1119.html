3. Home-court advantage — pinned to the measured home-win rate

The brief asks for HCA to be pinned rather than fitted. It is, and the pin is arithmetic anyone can repeat from the standings table above: sum every club's printed home W-L. A neutral-site game is not a home game, so neutral games are excluded from both sides of the fraction.

221home games
0.5023measured home-win rate
2neutral games excluded

sum of every club's printed home W-L; a neutral-site game is not a home game. nba.com/standings

Irregularity worth a reviewer's attention. The measured home-win rate on this date is 0.5023 — statistically indistinguishable from a coin flip over 221 games (a 95% band of about ±0.066 at n=221). That does not mean home court is worthless; it means this sample cannot separate it from zero, and an earlier build that pinned HCA from a smaller sample would have inherited the same problem. The pin is reported as measured, with its band, instead of being tuned until it looked right.

4. The two estimators, and their disagreement as the headline

Two ratings of the same club are computed from two different official pages, and the confidence signal published here is how far apart they land, not a blend of them:

  • Estimator A — points differential per game: own points per game (traditional page) minus opponent points per game (opponent page).
  • Estimator B — NetRtg converted to a per-game margin: net rating per 100 possessions (advanced page) × pace ÷ 100.

They share a source of truth by construction (they are the same season's results), so agreement is not independent confirmation of the model — it is a transcription and arithmetic check plus a style-dependence check, and it is labelled that way rather than dressed up as a second opinion. The largest disagreements are printed first below, because a club whose two estimators disagree is a club whose number should not be trusted to two decimals.
