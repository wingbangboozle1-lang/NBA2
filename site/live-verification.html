5. Why no profit number appears anywhere

no row in picks.json carries a market price (`american_odds` is absent from every signal), so profit is not measurable and is not published. A PnL here would be an assumed -110, i.e. an invented number.

A PnL would require assuming a price, and the assumption would be doing all the work: at −110 a 4-0 record is 3.64 units, at +150 it is 6.00. Publishing either would be publishing the assumption, not the model. The site therefore reports what was measured — wins, losses, error — and says plainly that the money question is unanswered until a price exists.

6. The verdict this page supports

the first genuine out-of-sample forecast the repository has: every graded pick won, but only four distinct games were bet, the model called the winner in 5 of 9 games (a naive net-rating baseline also got 5, always-home gets 7), and no sample this small can separate skill from luck.

Two conclusions follow, and they point in opposite directions, which is why both are printed:

  • Against the model: on nine games it named the winner no better than a naive net-rating baseline and worse than always picking the home team. No edge is demonstrated.
  • For the process: the gate machinery did its job. Every graded pick won, and the site still refuses to rank the strategy, because four games is not a sample. A system that can report 4-0 and simultaneously refuse to be impressed by it is the only kind worth building.
