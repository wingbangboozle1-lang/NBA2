Picks & the upcoming slate

Every game in the dated window through three days after the snapshot (2026-11-14 → 2026-11-17) is priced from our own originated lines, and every game gets a lean. What no game gets is a stake: a bet needs a price to beat and a settled sample large enough that the edge is not a coin flip that happened to land, and this repository has neither.

What a "lean" is here. It is the side our own number favours, together with the trigger — the decimal price at which backing it stops being a losing bet. At our probability p, break-even is decimal 1/p exactly, so the trigger is arithmetic, not a prediction about what a bookmaker will offer.
Two markets, two probabilities — read the right trigger. The lean is a spread at our own number, so the probability that prices it is the probability of covering that number (P(cover)), not the probability of winning the game (P(win)). Our line is the median of our own distribution, so P(cover) is close to 0.50 on every game by construction while P(win) ranges widely. Pricing a spread bet off P(win) — as an earlier build of this page did — produces a trigger of, say, −1246 on a moneyline-shaped probability, which no sportsbook offers on a spread. Both numbers are now printed, each with its own break-even trigger, so the arithmetic can be checked against whichever market is actually being considered.
0 of 27 games are staked. Not because the model failed, but because gate G6 (no market price) and gate G7 (167 settled bets against a 300-bet bar) both fail. Publishing a stake anyway is the one thing this build refuses to do.
