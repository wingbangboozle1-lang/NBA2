Calibration & reliability

Why 64.5% accuracy and a log-loss worse than a coin are the same fact about the same model.

The model's average distance from a coin flip is 0.277. Reality's is 0.500. The model is not wrong about who wins as often as it is wrong about how sure it is.

Accuracy only asks whether the larger probability was on the right side. Log-loss asks whether the probability was right. A model can pass the first and fail the second, and this one does.

The correction, measured on data it was not fitted to

Shrink the probability toward a coin flip: p′ = 0.5 + α(p − 0.5). Fitting α on the first 110 games (2026-10-25..2026-11-09) picks α = 0.55. On the untouched 42 held-out games that moves log-loss from 0.7082 to 0.6398 — a gain of +0.0684 and, for the first time, a score better than a coin (0.6931).

The gain is not a spike in the grid: every α from 0.30 to 0.80 beats a coin on the holdout. A correction that only worked at one value would be a fitted artefact.

Caveat, stated on the page rather than in a footnote. α is itself a fitted parameter. It is fitted on a different slice from the one it is scored on, which is what makes the gain evidence, but it is not a derivation. A second season would test whether 0.55 is a property of this model or of this month.

The same correction on the margin

Margins are over-dispersed too: the model's forecast spread is 13.389 points where the truth's is 16.788. Shrinking the margin by α = 0.45, fitted on the first 110 games, moves held-out MAE from 14.764 to 13.51 against a tie guess at 14.5.

So the raw forecast is worse than guessing a tie and the shrunk forecast is better than it. Both sentences are true and the site prints both, because publishing only the second would overstate the model and publishing only the first would understate it.

What this does and does not license

It licenses the projection surface: published probabilities are shipped shrunk, with the raw number beside them.

It does not license a bet. Beating a coin on log-loss is not beating a price; the price is absent, so the bet surface stays at zero stakes.
