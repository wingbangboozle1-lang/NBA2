What an injury actually does to a line — and what still cannot be said

Three things are needed to turn "Out" into points: the player's share of minutes, who replaces him, and how the team has actually performed with and without him. The first two are now measured above for three clubs from official inputs. The third — on/off, plus any per-game distribution — still needs the game log, and the game-log tab returns "No data available" server-side (verified at https://www.nba.com/stats/player/1630162/gamelog on 2026-11-14), so nothing below this point prints a projected line move.

Why the earlier build was right to leave these columns blank. At that build the player table was believed unreachable, so any production number would have been invented. The endpoint is reachable, so the numbers above are published from it — and everything the endpoint does not give (distributions, on/off, minutes floors) is still blank, still flagged, and still named in (F-061).

Game-flow risk created by availability

  • Blowout risk: a team missing four or more rotation players is more likely to be on the wrong end of a garbage-time game, which flattens late-game totals. Measurable from rotation counts; not yet quantified.
  • Back-to-back filing risk: the 1 p.m. game-day deadline for the second of a back-to-back means late scratches are structurally more likely there. The schedule in this build identifies those games; the effect needs settlement data.
  • Last-minute scratches: the highest-information signal in a day is the diff between consecutive reports. Only one report is stored, so no diff is possible yet (SG-10).
