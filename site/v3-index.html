NBA2 v3 — projections from two estimators

Originated lines from official NBA sources, with the disagreement between two independent estimators as the primary confidence signal, and a bet surface that stays empty until its gates pass.

Read this first. The v2 site's headline was its accuracy. This site's headline is how much the two estimators disagree, and the accuracy is printed underneath with the number that contradicts it. On the held-out slice the raw model's log-loss (0.7082) is worse than a coin (0.6931); the same model with its over-confidence removed (×0.55) scores 0.6398 and beats a coin. Both numbers are published; neither replaces the other.

What the two estimators do

A — running point differential. Each club's rating is the mean of its home and road scoring margins, shrunk toward the league mean. It reads only the scoreboard.

B — least-squares strength with home court absorbed. An exact constrained least-squares fit (ridge 0.5, ratings summing to zero) on the same games, where home advantage is a fitted term rather than a constant added afterwards.

The two are deliberately built from different assumptions. Where they agree, the line is as confident as this model gets; where they disagree, the disagreement is printed and the line's grade drops. The blend is not the headline: it appears on the lines page in a column headed “fallback only” for the case where a reader needs one number.

Calibration — home advantage pinned, not fitted

Home-court advantage is pinned to the measured home-win rate, as the brief requires: 0.5294 home win rate over 187 games gives h = σ·Φ⁻¹(p) = +0.963 points with σ=13.05 (SE 0.0366). The unconstrained fit is also reported — σ=8.74, h=+8.38, RMSE 1.0529 vs 1.055 — and it is not the published number, because a free h would absorb whatever the ratings get wrong and call it home court.
