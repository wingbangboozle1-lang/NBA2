0b. What this session (arena/766d0269-nba2) added, 2026-11-14 evening

Live re-verification passed on every row that was checked. The 30 club records on this site were re-read from nba.com/standings and matched exactly (30/30). The 27 window games were re-checked field by field against nba.com/schedule — date, tip time, away, home, game id and venue — and matched on every field (27/27). The scripts and the raw captures are in audit/2026-11-14-766d/ so the check can be repeated without trusting this page.
A published number was wrong, and is now fixed with a test that fails if it returns. The picks table priced a spread lean with the moneyline probability. Our spread is the median of our own distribution, so the probability of covering it is ~0.50 on every game, while the probability of winning the game ranges from 0.07 to 0.95. Pricing one with the other produced break-even triggers like −1246 — a price no sportsbook offers on a spread. Both probabilities and both triggers are now published side by side, and four regression tests pin the arithmetic.
The skill claim does not survive its own test — and that is the most useful result here. The model's 64.7% accuracy on 167 graded games is not distinguishable from the naive rule "back the team with the better record", which scored 70.3% on the same games. On the 33 games where they disagreed, the naive rule won 20 and the model 13 (exact McNemar p = 0.2962). Full arithmetic on Skill vs luck. The consequence for decisions is direct: any promotion rule that accepts accuracy alone would promote a strategy that has demonstrated nothing.
Eight decisions are now listed in one place, each with what we have, what is missing, how the gap could be closed and who must decide: Decisions needed. Three of them are blocked on data this repository does not hold, and the routes to obtain it are named.

1. Six things you need to know, in the order they affect a decision

What was built. A line-origination model over a rolling slate (2026-11-16 → 2026-11-18 published, 11/19 captured) with two independent estimators whose disagreement is the headline, a total modelled as possessions × points per possession, a home-court term pinned to the measured home-win rate rather than fitted, and source-linked tables throughout. Three sealed ledgers (22, 27 and 34 picks) are graded on one settlement surface, and the projection and bet surfaces are separated in code.
What is verified. The 2026-11-16 standings, schedule, three team-stat pages and injury releases were re-read from official NBA.com sources; all 30 standings rows and all 8 games of the 11/16 slate match the committed capture exactly. Both stored injury releases parse with zero unread cells. Every pre-tip ledger digest is recomputed before any grading runs. These are point-in-time captures with their own retrieval stamps, not a claim of live freshness.
What changed on 2026-11-16, and why it matters. 1) The injury table had been a hand transcription, and it was wrong on six rows: Cleveland's Tyson, Jaylon was published Questionable where the official PDF prints Doubtful, and five Golden State statuses were published blank although the PDF carried them. The releases are now stored verbatim and parsed by content type. 2) Two settlements disagreed and one graded a third of the picks; all three seals are now graded on one table. 3) The nightly job had failed 5 of 5 runs at a single step, which skipped the injury archive, the rolling refresh and the gates; each official surface now runs, and reports, independently.
The settled record, quoted honestly. 37 sealed rows are graded, but they are only 14 DISTINCT games — three seals priced overlapping dates and 23 gradings are repeats. On the distinct games the seals picked 12 correct winners against 8.855 expected under their own probabilities. The Poisson-binomial chance of doing at least that well is 0.0425: just outside the noise band, on fourteen games in one week, driven by the v3 seal's 9/9 on heavy favourites. It is not a measured edge and nothing is staked on it.
What is not possible yet. No licensed market price exists, so there is no edge, no CLV, no ROI and no stake sizing: the bet surface publishes zero rows and 29 explicit gate refusals. Player props need a prop feed and player-level production, neither of which is reachable from the build. The original injury PDFs are not in the repository because the sandbox cannot download them; the archive now runs from a runner and records its result.
The skill finding. A walk-forward test on official finals (every rating built only from strictly earlier games) puts the rating method at 67.7% straight-up (95% CI 60.2-74.3%), margin MAE 11.96 against 13.36 for a zero baseline, Brier 0.2170 against 0.2500 for a coin flip. That is predictive signal, not profit: with no price to beat, no ROI exists.
The compression finding. The same test shows predicted margins compressed to about a third of true dispersion and correlating with the real margin at only about +0.39. The model ranks games; it does not forecast blowouts, and no stake may be sized from a predicted margin.
Team-level luck. Across 30 teams, zero are outside the |z| ≤ 1.96 luck band, so no strategy may lean on an over- or under-performing narrative. That is the honest answer rather than a manufactured signal.
The correlation finding. Measured on official finals, same-game spread vs total r = -0.038 (95% CI -0.177 to +0.103): indistinguishable from zero, so every parlay is priced with the measured rho and the naive product printed beside it. Every player-prop correlation is still unmeasurable.
The recommended path. 1) Let the repaired nightly job run twice and read data/raw/refresh_verdict.json and data/raw/egress_probe.json — they answer, with a date, what a runner can reach. 2) Settle the 11/16 slate from official finals so the sample grows past fourteen games. 3) Only then consider a licensed odds feed, which is the single input that turns projections into decisions. Keep the leaderboard unrewarded until the gates measure on a settled sample.

Recommended sequence

  1. Stabilize official ingestion (SG-1 / SG-31). Diagnose the 2026-11-14 final-scores fetch failure, retain runner logs and alert on failures. Do not claim fresh data until a run succeeds end to end.
  2. Automate the rolling slate. Add a separate scheduled job to refresh official schedule and team inputs, generate the next window through no more than three days ahead, run provenance/freshness gates, then publish. The current workflow only refreshes dates already in sealed ledgers.
  3. Connect licensed odds (SG-3) before any bet, ROI, CLV or reward claim. Keep every stake at zero until a timestamped price exists and all validation gates pass.
  4. Acquire player game logs (SG-36), then measure player-level correlations, minute redistribution and prop probabilities. Keep these fields blank until sourced data is available.

Historical results support backtesting, but unattended freshness and next-slate origination are not yet verified. Existing gates, suggestions and questions remain the review path; no model output is promoted automatically.
