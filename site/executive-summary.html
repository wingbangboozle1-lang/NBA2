0b. What this session (arena/766d0269-nba2) added, 2026-11-14 evening

Live re-verification passed on every row that was checked. The 30 club records on this site were re-read from nba.com/standings and matched exactly (30/30). The 27 window games were re-checked field by field against nba.com/schedule — date, tip time, away, home, game id and venue — and matched on every field (27/27). The scripts and the raw captures are in audit/2026-11-14-766d/ so the check can be repeated without trusting this page.
A published number was wrong, and is now fixed with a test that fails if it returns. The picks table priced a spread lean with the moneyline probability. Our spread is the median of our own distribution, so the probability of covering it is ~0.50 on every game, while the probability of winning the game ranges from 0.07 to 0.95. Pricing one with the other produced break-even triggers like −1246 — a price no sportsbook offers on a spread. Both probabilities and both triggers are now published side by side, and four regression tests pin the arithmetic.
The skill claim does not survive its own test — and that is the most useful result here. The model's 64.7% accuracy on 167 graded games is not distinguishable from the naive rule "back the team with the better record", which scored 70.3% on the same games. On the 33 games where they disagreed, the naive rule won 20 and the model 13 (exact McNemar p = 0.2962). Full arithmetic on Skill vs luck. The consequence for decisions is direct: any promotion rule that accepts accuracy alone would promote a strategy that has demonstrated nothing.
Eight decisions are now listed in one place, each with what we have, what is missing, how the gap could be closed and who must decide: Decisions needed. Three of them are blocked on data this repository does not hold, and the routes to obtain it are named.

1. Six things you need to know, in the order they affect a decision

What was built. A v2 line-origination model over a 27-game snapshot from 2026-11-14 through 2026-11-17 (ending three days after the snapshot date), with two ratings estimators, a fitted market-agnostic total model, published formulas, and source-linked tables. A strategy lab evaluates seven candidate strategies and twelve validation gates, and separates observed results from luck with statistical tests.
What is verified. In the dated 2026-11-14 snapshot, 27/27 games match the official schedule; 30 team records match the standings capture and rebuild from the 183 committed official final scores; 26 injury rows are transcribed from the official 11/14 04:30 AM ET PDF. Team scoring, opponent scoring and advanced ratings are attributed to NBA.com Stats. These are point-in-time captures, not a claim of current live freshness.
What is not possible yet. No market price exists, so nothing can be staked and no ROI or CLV can be measured. The committed history contains 183 official finals through 2026-11-13, which supports historical backtests; it does not itself guarantee ongoing updates. The latest scheduled ingest run failed at the final-scores fetch before settlement or rebuild (F-043), so freshness is not assured. The strategy lab has 167 graded games against a 300-sample gate.
The skill finding. A walk-forward test on 167 official finals (2026-10-23 → 2026-11-13, every rating built only from strictly earlier games) puts this rating method at 67.7% straight-up (95% CI 60.2-74.3%), margin MAE 11.96 against 13.36 for a zero baseline, Brier 0.2170 against 0.2500 for a coin flip. A paired bootstrap over 4,000 draws puts P(MAE gain > 0) at 0.9998. That is real predictive signal. It is accuracy, NOT profit: there is still no price to beat, so no ROI exists.
The compression finding. The same test shows predicted margins are compressed to 32.6% of true dispersion (sd 5.44 vs 16.67) and correlate with the real margin at only +0.39. The model ranks games; it does not forecast blowouts, and no stake may ever be sized from a predicted margin.
The luck finding. Of the seven candidate strategies in the lab, three are distinguishable from chance at 95% and three are too small to call. The most eye-catching record on the site — S4 going 7 for 7 — is one of the three that cannot be called, and the leaderboard refuses to rank it. Separating skill from variance is now a gate (G9), not an opinion.
Team-level luck. Across 30 teams, zero are outside the |z| ≤ 1.96 luck band. No team in this window can honestly be called over- or under-performing its underlying scoring level, so no strategy may lean on that story.
The correlation finding. Measured on 30 teams: net points vs NetRtg r = +0.999; either vs win% r ≈ +0.93. Measured on 155 finals: spread vs total r = +0.018 (95% CI -0.15 to +0.18), i.e. indistinguishable from zero. Still unmeasurable: every player prop correlation, because no player box scores are reachable.
The recommended path. First investigate the failed scheduled ingest (F-043), then automate the next rolling slate from timestamped official schedule and team inputs. Historical results exist, but freshness is not assured and the current job does not originate a future window. A licensed odds feed is still required for edge, ROI, CLV or stakes; player game logs are required for props and quantified injury impacts. Keep the leaderboard unrewarded until these inputs and the strategy-specific validation gates pass.

Recommended sequence

  1. Stabilize official ingestion (SG-1 / SG-31). Diagnose the 2026-11-14 final-scores fetch failure, retain runner logs and alert on failures. Do not claim fresh data until a run succeeds end to end.
  2. Automate the rolling slate. Add a separate scheduled job to refresh official schedule and team inputs, generate the next window through no more than three days ahead, run provenance/freshness gates, then publish. The current workflow only refreshes dates already in sealed ledgers.
  3. Connect licensed odds (SG-3) before any bet, ROI, CLV or reward claim. Keep every stake at zero until a timestamped price exists and all validation gates pass.
  4. Acquire player game logs (SG-36), then measure player-level correlations, minute redistribution and prop probabilities. Keep these fields blank until sourced data is available.

Historical results support backtesting, but unattended freshness and next-slate origination are not yet verified. Existing gates, suggestions and questions remain the review path; no model output is promoted automatically.
