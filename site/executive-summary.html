1. Six things you need to know, in the order they affect a decision

What was built. A v2 line-origination model over a 27-game, three-day window (2026-11-14 → 2026-11-17), originated only from official NBA inputs, with two independent ratings estimators, a fitted market-agnostic total model, published formulas, and a per-row source link on every table. A strategy lab scores seven candidate strategies walk-forward, evaluates twelve validation gates from data, and separates luck from skill with binomial tests.
What is verified. 27/27 games match the official schedule; all 30 team records match live nba.com/standings exactly AND rebuild exactly from the 183 committed official final scores, home and road splits included; 26 injury rows are transcribed from the official 11/14 04:30 AM ET PDF; team scoring, opponent scoring and advanced ratings are official nba.com/stats tables.
What is not possible yet. No price exists, so nothing can be staked. The results history is now complete — 183 official finals, 2026-10-20 → 2026-11-13, with the count independently confirmed by the standings — so backtests, out-of-sample splits and settlement all run. Every game still gets a lean and a trigger price rather than a stake, because a stake needs a price to beat and 167 settled bets against a 300-bet bar.
The skill finding. A walk-forward test on 167 official finals (2026-10-23 → 2026-11-13, every rating built only from strictly earlier games) puts this rating method at 67.7% straight-up (95% CI 60.2-74.3%), margin MAE 11.96 against 13.36 for a zero baseline, Brier 0.2170 against 0.2500 for a coin flip. A paired bootstrap over 4,000 draws puts P(MAE gain > 0) at 0.9998. That is real predictive signal. It is accuracy, NOT profit: there is still no price to beat, so no ROI exists.
The compression finding. The same test shows predicted margins are compressed to 32.6% of true dispersion (sd 5.44 vs 16.67) and correlate with the real margin at only +0.39. The model ranks games; it does not forecast blowouts, and no stake may ever be sized from a predicted margin.
The luck finding. Of the seven candidate strategies in the lab, three are distinguishable from chance at 95% and three are too small to call. The most eye-catching record on the site — S4 going 7 for 7 — is one of the three that cannot be called, and the leaderboard refuses to rank it. Separating skill from variance is now a gate (G9), not an opinion.
The luck finding. Across 30 teams, zero are outside the |z| ≤ 1.96 luck band. No team in this window can honestly be called over- or under-performing its underlying scoring level, so no strategy may lean on that story.
The correlation finding. Measured on 30 teams: net points vs NetRtg r = +0.999; either vs win% r ≈ +0.93. Measured on 155 finals: spread vs total r = +0.018 (95% CI -0.15 to +0.18), i.e. indistinguishable from zero. Still unmeasurable: every player prop correlation, because no player box scores are reachable.
The recommended path. Licensed odds remain the only hard blocker for anything involving money. Scheduled ingestion keeps the snapshot fresh; per-game results are now partially available (19 games still missing). With odds plus 300 settled bets the leaderboard becomes computable; until then the honest product is a projection desk that grades its own lines.

Recommended sequence

  1. Connect the odds feed (SG-3). Nothing that involves money moves without a price. All other model work is preparation until then.
  2. Connect a per-game results feed (SG-2). This turns the model into something testable and lets the leaderboard exist.
  3. Schedule the ingestion (SG-1). Freshness gate G11 can never pass while data arrives by hand.
  4. Then measure the six missing correlations and re-price parlays; then pre-register strategies and start the competition.

Everything else on this site — gates, leaderboard rules, injury tracking, game flow — is designed to be ready when those three feeds land.
