Home court, pinned

Home advantage is pinned to the measured home-win rate rather than fitted: 0.5294 home-win rate over 187 games (SE 0.0366), hence h = σ·Φ⁻¹(p) = +0.963 points at σ = 13.05.

Why the unconstrained fit is not used. The unconstrained fit buys an RMSE of 1.0529 against the pinned 1.055 — a gain of 0.0022 — by claiming a home-win rate of 0.8312 against the measured 0.5294. That is the whole argument for pinning: the free parameter spends its freedom on a home rate no season has ever produced, and the RMSE gain is too small to distinguish from noise.

The free fit returns h = +8.38 at σ = 8.74, which implies a home-win rate of 0.8312 against the measured 0.5294. It is a worse model of reality that scores marginally better on one statistic, and it is not the published number.

Two estimators per club

Rating A is the shrunk mean scoring margin; rating B is the least-squares strength. They are on the same scale, so their difference is meaningful in points. The largest disagreement on this page is a club whose season-long margin and whose opponent-adjusted strength tell different stories — usually a club with a lopsided schedule or a few blowouts.
