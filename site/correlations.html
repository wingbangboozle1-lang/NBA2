Why you may not multiply the legs

A two-leg parlay's fair price is the joint probability, not the product. The arithmetic below is exact and needs no data — it is the part of parlay analysis that survives missing inputs.

two independent legs, p = 0.50 each      →  joint = 0.2500
same two legs, correlation ρ = +0.40     →  joint = 0.3200   (a 28% higher price)
same two legs, correlation ρ = −0.40     →  joint = 0.1800   (a 28% lower price)

Under-independence error = p_ind × δ, where (1+δ) is the factor by which the true joint
probability differs from the product. A parlay priced off the product is therefore mispriced
by exactly the correlation it ignored.

The consequence. At -110 a single leg needs 52.38% to break even; two legs need 27.44% jointly. If your legs are correlated you must know how correlated, because the same two 50% legs are worth 25.0% or 32.0% depending only on that number.
