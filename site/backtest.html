Backtest & measured skill

The one question every other page depends on: if this rating method had been run before each game, would it have been right? This is a walk-forward test on official finals only, with no leakage and no future information.

How the test works. 148 games (2026-10-23 → 2026-11-13) are scored one at a time. For each game the ratings are rebuilt from only the games strictly before that date — no same-day leakage, no future leakage — with the same shrinkage the published model uses. 16 games are skipped because a team had never played. Sources: nba.com/schedule for the slate and one nba.com/game/<slug>-<id> URL per final score.

The uncomfortable part: the model is compressed

Do not read a predicted margin as an expected margin. The model's predicted margins have a standard deviation of 5.17 pts against an actual 16.51 pts — a ratio of 0.31. Its correlation with the real margin is only +0.390, and a bigger predicted margin says almost nothing about how big the real win will be (Spearman +0.088). The ratings rank teams usefully; they do not forecast blowouts. Anything on this site that turned a predicted margin into a stake size would be misusing the model.

Calibration: are the probabilities right?

A model can rank well and still be badly calibrated. These bins compare predicted win probability with the observed rate, with a Wilson 95% interval so a small bin is not over-read.

Calibration slope 0.27. A perfectly calibrated model has a slope of 1.00; below 1 means it is over-confident in the sense that its probabilities spread wider than reality, above 1 means under-confident. The slope here is pulled down by the compression above: the model's margins are too small-scaled, so its probabilities bunch near 50% and must be rescaled before they mean anything. 131 more games at this accuracy would be needed to prove an edge over a 54.2% coin-flip.

Does a bigger predicted margin mean a bigger real win?

Only weakly. Rank correlation between |predicted margin| and |actual margin| is +0.088 — essentially no relationship. The model's confidence is a ranking device, not a magnitude forecast.

Bottom line. On 148 official finals this method is 66.2% accurate straight up (CI 58.3–73.3%), beats a zero-margin baseline by 10.1% on MAE, and its probabilities score a Brier of 0.2185 against 0.2500 for a coin flip. That is real signal. It is not profit: there is no price to beat in this repository, so no ROI, no CLV, and no rank on the leaderboard can be produced from it. The leaderboard stays empty until an odds feed and 300 settled bets exist (G1, G6).
